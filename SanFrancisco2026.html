The trajectory

But this is changing fast — don’t bet on “now”

METR, Task-Completion Time Horizons (metr.org/time-horizons, May 2026 snapshot, CC-BY) — Claude Mythos Preview at ≥16 h.

Exponential improvement

AI task horizons are doubling every ~105 days (~1020%/year, METR TH 1.1, 2024–2026 window). Today’s “noisy” or “mildly useful” outputs won’t stay that way. Build for where models will be in a year, not where they are today.

AI co-scientist systems become obsolete really fast

Denario, Kosmos, Sakana — all tightly coupled to yesterday’s models. Denario is already obsolete 6 months later. As models improve, these AI co-scientist systems are replaced wholesale.

The question

What’s the right thing
to build right now?

Not for today’s noisy demos — for the agents we’ll have in a year.

Two direct implications

If both of those things are true…

Output rate goes up

AI-assisted research will produce more analyses, faster, with smaller teams — and the curve does not flatten in the next five years.

Trust must scale with it

We need infrastructure — not goodwill — that lets readers and machines verify what an analysis actually did.

The bottleneck stops being how much we can produce. It becomes how much we can trust.

Our thesis

The paper isn’t enough anymore.

We need a substrate that’s legible to both humans and agents.

A research project should be a structured artifact — inputs, decisions, outputs, evidence, narrative — that an agent can write and a reader can audit, line by line.

Inspectable

Every claim links to the artifact that supports it.

Reproducible

Every output is a recipe — the analysis re-runs from the spec.

Composable

Today’s analysis is tomorrow’s input — trust transfers.

The impact

Science that actually compounds.

Right now, every paper restarts the loop — new code, new conventions, new figures, new audit. Trust resets to zero with each PDF.

With a structured substrate, results stack. A new study builds on artifacts you can already check — and the next agent picks up where the last one left off.

The unit of science
becomes the artifact,
not the PDF.

Inspectable, reproducible, composable — and finally, cumulative.

Part 2 · the substrate

ASTRA

Agentic Schema for Transparent Research Analysis

An open specification for structuring computational research — inspectable, reproducible, legible to humans and agents alike.

ASTRA · the shape

Five things, every project declares.

The whole spec fits on one slide. That’s on purpose.

Inputs

Data — or another ASTRA project. Analyses chain.

Outputs

Metrics, figures, tables, reports — each with a recipe.

Decisions

Every choice point — with options, default, rationale.

Evidence

Claims tied to verbatim quotes or produced artifacts.

Narrative

Prose anchored to the spec by inline links.

Written in plain YAML + Markdown. Validateable. Diffable. Composable.

ASTRA · what changes

From PDF to artifact.

Today

  • A polished PDF, opaque.
  • Code lives somewhere on a private cluster.
  • Every figure is its own untracked snowflake.
  • Reproduction is a side project for whoever’s curious.
  • Robustness checks are a discretionary appendix.

With ASTRA

  • One open spec — the project, end to end.
  • Containers and recipes travel with the analysis.
  • Every figure traces back to an exact configuration.
  • Reproduction is one command.
  • A new robustness check is five lines of YAML.

Part 4 · the initiative

Lightcone Research

An open-source initiative building the substrate for AI-powered science.

Lightcone Research · the stack

What we’re building — in the open.

Three layers, all open-source, all interoperable.

The spec

ASTRA — the open standard. YAML + Markdown, with a validator and a reference toolchain.

The viewer

A narrative interface for ASTRA projects — readable like a paper, inspectable like a notebook.

The skills

Harness-agnostic agent skills that read & write ASTRA — usable from Claude Code, Cursor, or your own loop.

Specifications are open. Tooling is permissively licensed. The substrate belongs to the community.
